# Writing FAQs that get used

Write FAQs that get used by basing them on the real questions people ask, phrasing each question the way a person actually would, and answering it directly and completely in the first sentence. The same direct-answer writing that makes content citable is what makes an FAQ worth marking up.

## Use real questions

The questions in an FAQ should be the ones people actually ask, phrased in their words. Real questions, drawn from what your audience genuinely wants to know, match the queries people type and the questions AI systems field. Invented or self-serving questions that no one asks do not match real demand and do not get used. Sourcing the questions from actual audience questions is what makes the FAQ relevant.

## Phrase questions naturally

Each question should be phrased the way a person would actually ask it, in natural language, because that is what matches a real query. A question written in stiff or keyword-stuffed phrasing matches poorly; one written as a person would say it matches the queries it is meant to answer. The closer the question's wording is to how people really ask, the better the FAQ connects to the people asking. Write the questions in human language.

## Answer directly and first

Answer each question directly in the first sentence, then add any needed detail after. An answer that opens with the actual answer, rather than building up to it, is what an engine can extract and cite and what a reader wants. The direct-answer-first pattern, the same one that makes any content citable, applies squarely to FAQ answers. Lead with the answer; the FAQ format makes this discipline natural, since each answer has a clear question to address.

## Make answers self-contained

Each answer should make sense on its own, without depending on the other questions for context, because an engine may extract a single question-answer pair in isolation. A self-contained answer that names its subject and stands alone survives extraction; one that relies on surrounding answers does not. Writing each pair to be complete by itself is what lets individual FAQ entries be used and cited independently. Treat each question-answer pair as a standalone unit.
