# Common mistakes

The common FAQ schema mistakes are markup that does not match the visible content, invented questions no one asks, answers that bury the response or depend on context, invalid syntax, and using the markup on content that is not genuinely a FAQ. Each either wastes the markup or risks it being disregarded.

## Mismatch with the page

The most serious mistake is schema that describes questions or answers not actually visible on the page, or that differ from what users see. This violates the guidelines and can lead the engine to ignore or penalize the markup. The schema must mirror the visible FAQ. Marking up hidden or different content, even with good intentions, breaks the rule that markup describes real, user-facing content. Keep them identical.

## Invented questions

Filling an FAQ with questions no one actually asks, to manufacture content for the markup, produces an FAQ that matches no real demand and gets no use. The questions should come from what people genuinely ask. Invented, self-serving, or padding questions waste the effort and can make the page worse for users. Real questions are what give the FAQ and its markup value; invented ones give it neither relevance nor citations.

## Indirect or dependent answers

Answers that build up to the point instead of leading with it, or that only make sense alongside other answers, fail when an engine extracts a single pair. The answer should lead with the response and stand on its own. An answer that hides its substance or relies on context cannot be used in isolation, which defeats the purpose of marking up individual pairs. Direct and self-contained is what makes each answer usable.

## Invalid or misapplied markup

Markup with syntax errors may be ignored entirely, so failing to validate is a quiet but total failure. Applying FAQ schema to content that is not actually a question-and-answer format misuses the type and risks the same disregard. Both come down to the markup not being a correct, honest description of genuine FAQ content. Validating the syntax and using the type only for real Q&A content are the two checks that prevent the markup from being wasted or working against you.
